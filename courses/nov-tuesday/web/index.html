Section 01 · The Profile

Your profile is how opportunity finds you.

The Portal matches people to opportunity through skills. A profile that only repeats your job title keeps most of your range invisible, and ten minutes of honest listing fixes that.

The profile rule

List what you can do, wherever you learned it, including the skills your current role never asks for. The Portal matches on skills, so every one you leave off is a door that stays shut. What you want to build next belongs on there too.

Which profile gets found by the right opportunities?

Grounded in: Internal mobility and retention (LinkedIn Workplace Learning)

On your own: Write down one skill you use every week that your job title would never suggest.

Go deeper

Writing skills the Portal can match

There is a craft to naming a skill. 'Good with people' matches nothing; 'onboarding new hires' or 'de-escalating frustrated callers' matches real postings. Name the activity and keep it specific, splitting big skills into the pieces you actually do. Then add what you want to build next: that short list is how a stretch gig or a navigator finds you before you knew to look.

On your own

  1. Draft ten skills as activities: what you do, for whom, with what. The hidden ones show up after the first five.
  2. Pick two skills you want to grow next and add them to your profile as the direction you are building.

Section 02 · The Search

Search by the skills you want to use.

The Portal lists projects and short gigs alongside jobs. The World Economic Forum expects 39 percent of key skills to change by 2030, so search by the skills you want to use rather than your title.

Go deeper

The case for a six-week gig

A gig deserves more respect than it gets. Six weeks on another team answers questions no posting can: whether you enjoy the work daily, whether the team's rhythm suits you, whether the skill belongs at the center of your job. It also leaves evidence, a finished project and a manager in another building who has seen your work. Some of the strongest internal moves start as a gig both sides wanted to continue.

On your own

  1. Run one skill-first search filtered to gigs and short projects. Save anything that pulls at you, even slightly.
  2. For your favorite, write the question a navigator could answer about fitting the time alongside your current role.

Section 03 · In the Open

No secrecy required.

The old instinct says job searching means hiding. The Portal is built for the opposite: managers are expected to support moves, navigators exist to help, and every stage has a next step you can ask about. Read each situation and call the move.

Play · Call the move

Why this matters: Three situations from the weeks after you raise your hand. Call each one.

On your own: Name the old job-search habit that would be hardest for you to drop.

Go deeper

What happens after you raise your hand

After you express interest, the hiring team sees your profile and your raised hand, and the process moves through stages you are allowed to ask about: review, conversations, decision. None of it is secret and none of it is binding. Navigators sit outside the hiring team, so they can tell you the stage, coach your skills profile, and help you plan the manager conversation, with nothing counted against you for asking.

On your own

  1. Book 20 minutes with a navigator this month, even without a live application. Ask what strong first moves look like.
  2. Write the sentence you would use to tell your manager you expressed interest, and say it out loud once.
