Take-Off Tuesday · Tools and tech, hands on · Spot AI-Exposed Work

Sort the week, one task at a time.

Sort your work task by task, never by job. Each task gets one call: AI does it and you check, AI helps and you drive, or the task stays yours entirely.

By the end of this session, you will be able to

  1. Break your working week into specific, repeatable tasks with outputs and rhythms
  2. Sort each task into automate, augment, or leave alone, and know the tells for each
  3. Test one sort call this week in an approved VU tool, checking step included

Begin the session

Section 01 · The Breakdown

Break the week into sortable tasks.

Asked what you do, you say your title. The sort works a level down. Harvard's field experiment with consultants found AI lifted some tasks and degraded others for the same people, so write your week the way Tuesday actually went, task by task.

The decomposition rule

A sortable task names an output and a rhythm: the thing produced and how often. 'Communications' is a job. 'Draft the Thursday newsletter from submitted items' is a task, and a task can be sorted.

In the room

When work stays at the altitude of titles and projects, AI talk turns into dread about whole jobs. Nobody automates 'coordinator'; tools meet the Monday summary and the standard reply. Get your week written at that level and the fear turns into decisions you can actually make.

Talk it through: Describe last Tuesday to your neighbor as three tasks. Which one was hardest to name, and why had it gone invisible?

Quick check: which of these is ready to sort?

Grounded in: The jagged technological frontier, Dell'Acqua et al. (HBS)

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: turn 'my job' into three real tasks from this week, specific enough that someone else could picture doing them. Swap lists.

On your own: Write three real tasks from this week, specific enough that someone else could picture doing them.

Go deeper

The invisible half of your week

The fastest way past the blur is evidence. Your calendar and your sent folder remember Tuesday better than you do: standing meetings carry prep work, and the sent folder shows the same three replies leaving every week. Most people find a chunk of their week is repeating work they never named. That unnamed work goes to the top of the sort list, because unexamined routine is exactly what AI handles best.

On your own

  1. Open last week's calendar and sent folder. List every repeating piece of work you find, written as an output and a rhythm.
  2. Star the tasks you never think of as tasks: the prep, the formatting, the standard replies. Sort those first.

Section 02 · The Sort

Automate, augment, or leave alone.

Three calls, made per task. Automate: AI does it, you check the output. Augment: AI helps, you drive. Leave alone: human all the way, because the task runs on trust, judgment, or private information about people. The traffic light rides along on every call.

Play · Make the call

Why this matters: Sort each task and note the tell you used: the traffic-light color of the content, how fixed the format is, how much judgment the task needs.

Grounded in: Centaurs and cyborgs, Ethan Mollick, One Useful Thing

Breakout · 3 min: Breakout pairs: each pick one task from your list and make the call out loud. Partner gets one challenge question. 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Pick one task from your list and make the call: automate, augment, or leave alone. Write one line on why.

Go deeper

Centaur work, cyborg work, red thread

Ethan Mollick, who studies AI at Wharton, describes two ways to run an augment task. Centaurs draw a line down the middle: the tool drafts, the person decides. Cyborgs pass the work back and forth all day. Start centaur, because the line makes checking easy. And watch for the red thread, a task with private details about a person woven in. Those parts come out before any tool sees the rest, or the whole task stays human.

On your own

  1. Sort ten tasks from your week into the three buckets. Note which bucket filled up and whether that surprised you.
  2. Run one augment task centaur style: the tool drafts, you decide, and you note where the line sat. Move the line next time if it sat wrong.
  3. Find one task with a red thread and write which parts come out before a tool touches it.

Section 03 · The Move

Test one call this week.

Pick one automate or augment call and run it once this week: one task, one date, one approved VU tool, and a checking step you actually perform. Either result is useful. An hour saved confirms the call; a mess caught by your check says re-sort the task.

Practice · Set up the test

Why this matters: Choose the task and the checking step, then run the test. Notice what makes a test specific enough to teach you something by Friday.

Choose both steps
Go deeper

Read the test like evidence

A test deserves a fair reading. Count the whole cost: setup, prompting, and checking time against the old way, because a tool that saves forty minutes and needs thirty of checking earned ten. What your check caught becomes the permanent checklist for the task. A failed test is a real result too: write one line on why the call was wrong, then re-sort the task. Scale to a second task after two clean runs of the first.

On your own

  1. After the test, write three numbers: minutes the old way, minutes with the tool including the check, and errors the check caught.
  2. Turn what the check caught into a named checklist you run every time the task ships.

The rule to remember

What each part taught

1 · Break the week into sortable tasks.

A sortable task names an output and a rhythm: the thing produced and how often. 'Communications' is a job. 'Draft the Thursday newsletter from submitted items' is a task, and a task can be sorted.

Grounded in: The jagged technological frontier, Dell'Acqua et al. (HBS)

2 · Automate, augment, or leave alone.

You make the call cleanly, per task, with the traffic light in view.

Grounded in: Centaurs and cyborgs, Ethan Mollick, One Useful Thing

3 · Test one call this week.

A real result by Friday. You know exactly what the tool saved you and exactly what your check caught.

The recap, with the answers

1 · The sort is always made...

Answer: Per task, one call at a time

Jobs never sort cleanly; tasks do. Every job is a mix of automate, augment, and leave alone, and that mix is the honest picture.

2 · A first draft built from internal meeting notes is best sorted as...

Answer: Augment, in approved VU tools

Internal content is yellow light: approved VU tools only, with you driving. AI speeds the draft; your judgment makes it right.

3 · What makes an automate call safe?

Answer: A named checking step you actually run

Automate means AI does it AND you check. Names, numbers, and dates against the source: the check is half of the call, every time.

Your commitment

Pick the task your sort points to first. Task language only, no names of people; nothing you type is saved or sent.

  • The automate test. Run the task through an approved VU tool once, then check names, numbers, and dates against the source before anything ships.
  • The augment test. Use an approved VU tool for the first draft only, keep every decision yours, and note what the draft got wrong.
  • The leave-alone case. Write two lines on why this task stays human: the trust, judgment, or people data at its center. Naming that case is a skill too.
