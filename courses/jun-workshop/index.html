Begin the session

Section 01 · The Overlay

Lay both maps side by side.

Spread both artifacts: the exposure map of work that is changing, and the skills read of who could grow. The plan lives where they touch. Deloitte's skills-based research matches work to skills, never to job titles. Roles only, never names.

The overlay rule

An action-ready insight has two coordinates: a task whose shape is changing, and a strength or gap on the team it points at. One coordinate makes a slide. Two make a move you can hand an owner.

In the room

This table is holding a year of looking: the talent inventory habit from the fall, the task maps from the spring, the honest sorts in between. Most planning fails for lack of exactly what is in front of you now. In this room the raw material is done; today is for writing the moves.

Talk it through: Where do your two maps touch first? Name the task and the strength, roles only, and let the table say what move they see.

Quick check: which of these is action-ready?

Grounded in: The skills-based organization, a new model for work (Deloitte)

Breakout · 3 min: Point a neighbor at one place your maps touch: an exposed task near an underused strength, roles only. The neighbor says the move they see before you say yours.

Section 02 · The Build

Write the moves you can check.

The worksheet asks for two lines. The reskill line says who grows, toward what work, starting when. The second names a strength that moves or a role rebuilt around what stays human. Both lines end with an owner and a date.

Play · Grade the line

Why this matters: Three plan lines from tables like this one. Grade each before it goes home.

Breakout · 3 min: Write both lines, then read them to your table partner exactly as written. Anywhere they ask 'meaning what?', the line is still fog: rewrite it on the spot.

Section 03 · The Test

Test the plan out loud.

Last build of the year, out loud. Kotter's change research found efforts mostly fail in the telling, so your plan meets the table: managers asking what the owner needs and what the person whose work changes will hear. Then the first conversation gets booked.

Practice · Send the plan home

Why this matters: A plan survives on who hears it first and how its dates hold. Choose both.

Choose both steps

Grounded in: Leading change: why transformation efforts fail, John Kotter (HBR)

Breakout · 3 min: Read your two lines to the table. Listeners ask the card questions: what does the owner need, what will the person hear, what slips first? Revise on the spot.
