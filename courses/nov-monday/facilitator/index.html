05 · When things go sideways

If it breaks, you have a move

If Screen share or the site fails mid-session: Paste the learner link in chat and narrate from your own browser; every interaction runs on learner devices without the share.
If Breakout rooms are unavailable: Run the breakout prompts as 60-second chat storms: everyone types their answer at once, you read the best three aloud.
If A manager gets defensive about self-locating in a group setting: Remind the room nothing typed is saved or shared, and self-locates stay private unless someone volunteers. Today is a mirror, nothing more; the standard measures behavior going forward.
If Someone asks whether their self-locate feeds their performance review: Be direct: nothing from this session is captured anywhere. Goal 2 measurement runs through the formal process your HR partner owns; today is private practice at looking honestly.

06 · Tough questions, ready answers

Say each answer out loud once before you teach

Will my self-locate or anything from this session feed my performance review?

No. Nothing typed today is saved or sent, and the session has no reporting behind it. Goal 2 measurement runs through the formal process your HR partner owns; today is private practice at looking honestly before anyone else looks.

My team is huge and my calendar is brutal. Are these behaviors realistic for me?

The standard names behaviors, and behaviors scale: a monthly 1:1 exists whether you have three reports or fifteen, even if the shape differs. If a specific line truly cannot fit your context, that is a real conversation to have with your own leader, out loud, rather than a quiet exemption.

How is this different from the competency models we have seen before?

Competencies describe qualities and invite debate about whether you have them. The standard names witnessable behaviors, which ends the debate: either the 1:1 happened or it did not. That is also what makes Goal 2 measurable. The New Manager Foundations course on LinkedIn Learning walks the same ground if you want a deeper pass.

Section 01 · The List

Written so a colleague could watch it happen.

Plenty of leadership models describe who a manager should be. The Vanderbilt standard does something more useful: it lists what a manager here does, in lines a colleague could watch happen. That design choice is the whole point of this section.

The witness test

Every line of the Vanderbilt manager standard passes one test: a colleague could watch you do it. Holding the 1:1, giving written feedback, naming the expectation out loud. If nobody could witness it, it is not on the list.

Quick check: which of these is written the way the standard is written?

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: pick one line of the standard. When did a colleague last see you do it? 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Pick one line of the standard and write down when a colleague last saw you do it.

Facilitator scriptFull 8 min · Core 6

Establish that every line of the Vanderbilt manager standard is an observable, demonstrable behavior.

Say

“Vanderbilt wrote down what good managing looks like here, and the writing choice matters more than it seems. Every line is a behavior a colleague could watch you do: hold the 1:1, give the written feedback, name the expectation out loud. Nothing on the list asks you to be a certain kind of person. It asks you to do certain visible things, and that difference is the whole month.”

Do
  1. Read the concept card aloud once, slowly.
  2. Run the knowledge check; everyone answers on their own screen.
  3. Run the breakout: one line of the standard, and when a colleague last saw you do it.
Ask

“Why do you think the standard was written as behaviors instead of qualities?”

Expect:

  • Behaviors can be measured
  • Qualities are arguable, behaviors are checkable
  • It tells new managers exactly what to do

Respond: All of it. A quality invites debate; a behavior invites a calendar entry. Goal 2 can only measure what can be witnessed.

Debrief

If no colleague could witness it, it is not on the list. That single design choice is what makes the standard usable.

Watch for: Managers reading the list as an accusation. Frame it as a map: it tells you exactly what good looks like, which most of us were never told.

Transition

“So what counts as a behavior? That line is blurrier than it sounds.”

30-min core: Core: skip the breakout; ask for one surprising line of the standard in chat instead.

Section 02 · The Test

Grade yourself on what they could see.

Most of us grade ourselves on intentions and grade everyone else on behavior. I meant to give feedback. I am approachable. The witness test cuts through: could a colleague have seen it happen? Run it on three ordinary claims and watch the categories separate.

Play · Behavior or intention?

Why this matters: Three claims managers make about themselves. Run the witness test on each one and call it.

Breakout · 3 min: Breakout pairs: name one thing you give yourself credit for that fails the witness test. 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Name one thing you give yourself credit for that a colleague could never have witnessed.

Facilitator scriptFull 10 min · Core 7

Train the witness test: separating observable behaviors from intentions and personality traits.

Say

“Here is the trap this section exists to spring: most of us grade ourselves on intentions and grade everyone else on behavior. I meant to give feedback. I am an approachable person. The witness test cuts through all of it with one question: could a colleague have seen it happen? If the answer is no, it may be a lovely quality, and it is invisible to your team.”

Do
  1. Run Behavior or intention? live; everyone plays on their own screen.
  2. After round two, pause and ask who chose trait versus intention; the distinction sparks the best discussion.
  3. Run the breakout on credit that fails the witness test.
Ask

“What is something managers commonly claim that fails the witness test?”

Expect:

  • Having an open door
  • Caring about development
  • Being available anytime

Respond: Open door is the classic: it describes a state, and reports only experience what happens when they walk through it. The behavior version is scheduled, visible time.

Debrief

Intentions live in your head, traits live in adjectives, behaviors live on calendars and in records. The standard counts only the third kind.

Watch for: Cynicism about box-checking. Acknowledge it: a behavior done emptily is still weak managing. The standard is the floor that makes the rest possible.

Transition

“Which brings us to the uncomfortable part: where do you actually stand on it?”

30-min core: Core: run the trainer, skip the breakout, take one voice on the debrief question.

Section 03 · The Mirror

The mirror works when you bring evidence.

Nobody sees your answers and nothing you type is saved, so the only person you can fool here is you. An honest self-locate uses evidence: for each behavior, when did a colleague last see me do it? Then the biggest gap gets one visible move.

Practice · The honest self-locate

Why this matters: You are alone with the standard for five minutes. Choose how you rate yourself and what you do with the gap you find.

Choose both steps
Facilitator scriptFull 9 min · Core 6

Teach an evidence-based self-locate against the standard and convert the biggest gap into one visible move.

Say

“Now the mirror. Nobody sees your answers and nothing is saved, so there is no audience to perform for. The only way to waste the next five minutes is to be generous with yourself. For each behavior on the standard, ask one question: when did a colleague last actually see me do this? Then take the gap that matters most and give it a first visible move.”

Do
  1. Run the builder; give the room two minutes to make both choices and run it.
  2. Ask one volunteer to share which slot was harder to answer honestly.
  3. Point at the pattern: evidence over gut feel, one named behavior over a vague vow.
Ask

“Why does picking one behavior beat vowing to improve across the board?”

Expect:

  • One thing actually happens
  • Broad vows dissolve by Wednesday
  • You can put one thing on a calendar

Respond: Exactly. The standard has many lines; your November needs one. A single visible behavior, repeated, moves your read on the whole list.

Debrief

Honest self-locates run on evidence: when a colleague last saw it. The output is one behavior with its first instance on the calendar.

Watch for: Managers who self-locate low on everything and spiral. Redirect to the one gap that matters most; the standard is a growth map and everyone starts somewhere on it.
