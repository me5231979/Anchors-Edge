Goal 2, Manager Effectiveness at Scale.

Audience

When things go sideways

If Screen share or the site fails mid-session: Paste the learner link in chat and narrate from your own browser; every interaction runs on learner devices without the share.

If Breakout rooms are unavailable: Run the breakout prompts as 60-second chat storms: everyone types their answer at once, you read the best three aloud.

If A manager gets defensive about self-locating in a group setting: Remind the room nothing typed is saved or shared, and self-locates stay private unless someone volunteers. Today is a mirror, nothing more; the standard measures behavior going forward.

If Someone asks whether their self-locate feeds their performance review: Be direct: nothing from this session is captured anywhere. Goal 2 measurement runs through the formal process your HR partner owns; today is private practice at looking honestly.

Tough questions, ready answers

Q: Will my self-locate or anything from this session feed my performance review?
No. Nothing typed today is saved or sent, and the session has no reporting behind it. Goal 2 measurement runs through the formal process your HR partner owns; today is private practice at looking honestly before anyone else looks.

Q: My team is huge and my calendar is brutal. Are these behaviors realistic for me?
The standard names behaviors, and behaviors scale: a monthly 1:1 exists whether you have three reports or fifteen, even if the shape differs. If a specific line truly cannot fit your context, that is a real conversation to have with your own leader, out loud, rather than a quiet exemption.

Q: How is this different from the competency models we have seen before?
Competencies describe qualities and invite debate about whether you have them. The standard names witnessable behaviors, which ends the debate: either the 1:1 happened or it did not. That is also what makes Goal 2 measurable. The New Manager Foundations course on LinkedIn Learning walks the same ground if you want a deeper pass.

Behaviors, on the record Full 9 min · Core 6

Establish that every line of the Vanderbilt manager standard is an observable, demonstrable behavior.

Say

“Vanderbilt wrote down what good managing looks like here, and the writing choice matters more than it seems. Google did the same exercise years ago. Project Oxygen set out to learn what made its best managers good, and the answer came back as a list of teachable behaviors rather than personality types. Our standard works the same way: every line is something a colleague could watch you do. Nothing on it asks you to be a certain kind of person.”

Do

  1. Read the concept card aloud once, slowly.
  2. Walk the In the room context: being language versus doing language. No quiz in the live room.
  3. Open the discussion prompt; take two or three voices before moving on.
  4. Run the breakout: one line of the standard, and when a colleague last saw you do it.

Ask

“Why do you think the standard was written as behaviors instead of qualities?”

Expect:

  • Behaviors can be measured
  • Qualities are arguable, behaviors are checkable
  • It tells new managers exactly what to do

Respond: All of it. A quality invites debate; a behavior invites a calendar entry. Goal 2 can only measure what can be witnessed.

Debrief: If no colleague could witness it, it is not on the list. That single design choice is what makes the standard usable.

Watch for: Managers reading the list as an accusation. Frame it as a map: it tells you exactly what good looks like, which most of us were never told.

Transition: “So what counts as a behavior? That line is blurrier than it sounds.”

Core path: Core: skip the breakout; ask for one surprising line of the standard in chat instead.

Behavior or intention? Full 11 min · Core 8

Train the witness test: separating observable behaviors from intentions and personality traits.

Say

“Most of us grade ourselves on intentions and grade everyone else on behavior. I meant to give feedback. I am an approachable person. The witness test asks one question of every claim: could a colleague have seen it happen? If the answer is no, it may be a lovely quality, and your team has never met it.”

Do

  1. Run Behavior or intention? live; everyone plays on their own screen.
  2. After round two, pause and ask who chose trait versus intention; the distinction sparks the best discussion.
  3. Run the breakout on credit that fails the witness test.

Ask

“What is something managers commonly claim that fails the witness test?”

Expect:

  • Having an open door
  • Caring about development
  • Being available anytime

Respond: Open door is the classic: it describes a state, and reports only experience what happens when they walk through it. The behavior version is scheduled, visible time.

Debrief: Behaviors leave tracks on calendars and in records. Intentions and traits stay in your head, and the standard cannot count what stays there.

Watch for: Cynicism about box-checking. Acknowledge it: a behavior done emptily is still weak managing. The standard is the floor that makes the rest possible.

Transition: “Which brings us to the uncomfortable part: where do you actually stand on it?”

Core path: Core: run the trainer, skip the breakout, take one voice on the debrief question.

The honest self-locate Full 9 min · Core 6

Teach an evidence-based self-locate against the standard and convert the biggest gap into one visible move.

Say

“Now the mirror. Nobody sees your answers and nothing is saved, so there is no audience to perform for. The only way to waste the next five minutes is to be generous with yourself. For each behavior on the standard, ask one question: when did a colleague last actually see me do this? Then take the gap that matters most and give it a first visible move.”

Do

  1. Run the builder; give the room two minutes to make both choices and run it.
  2. Ask one volunteer to share which slot was harder to answer honestly.
  3. Point at the pattern: evidence over gut feel, one named behavior over a vague vow.

Ask

“Why does picking one behavior beat vowing to improve across the board?”

Expect:

  • One thing actually happens
  • Broad vows dissolve by Wednesday
  • You can put one thing on a calendar

Respond: Exactly. The standard has many lines; your December needs one. A single visible behavior, repeated, moves your read on the whole list.

Debrief: Honest self-locates run on evidence: when a colleague last saw it. The output is one behavior with its first instance on the calendar.

Watch for: Managers who self-locate low on everything and spiral. Redirect to the one gap that matters most; the standard is a growth map and everyone starts somewhere on it.

Transition: “Quick recap, then you build your own card.”

Core path: Core: everyone runs the builder solo; skip the share-out.
