The goal this month serves

Goal 1, Workforce Intelligence & Transfer Portal. Baseline 6% of BUs, Q4 target 85% of BUs.

Audience

Vanderbilt people managers and team leads in the Anchor's Edge weekly rhythm. Works for 6 to 40 on a call; breakouts run in pairs. No prerequisites; January opens the Retain, Grow, Move month on succession and internal mobility.

Room setup

Virtual, instructor led: camera on, deck link in chat, breakouts of 2 available. Learners follow on their own screens via the welcome-slide link or QR. Nothing typed into the deck is saved or sent.

Materials

  • Meeting platform with screen share and breakout rooms; deck link ready to paste in chat
  • Facilitator machine with the facilitator edition open; notifications off
  • Learners on their own devices with the learner link
  • Chat open for share-outs; the capstone copy button pastes there cleanly

A week before

  1. Run the learner edition start to finish and do every interaction honestly; you will demo at least one live.
  2. Read this month's theme page on the Anchor's Edge dashboard so the goal tie-in is fluent.
  3. Send the invite with the learner link and one line on what to bring.

The day before

  1. Test screen share with the facilitator edition; confirm the notes rail is readable.
  2. Open the learner link on a second device; the QR must land there.
  3. Run the section interactions once so you can drive them without reading.

30 minutes before

  1. Welcome slide up as people join; link pasted in chat.
  2. Close every other tab; silence the machine.
  3. Breakout rooms pre-configured in pairs.

When things go sideways

If Screen share or the site fails mid-session: Paste the learner link in chat and narrate from your own browser; every interaction runs on learner devices without the share.

If Breakout rooms are unavailable: Run the breakout prompts as 60-second chat storms: everyone types their answer at once, you read the best three aloud.

If A manager says naming a bench will make people expect promotions: Give the honest-language line: readiness and direction, never a guarantee. 'I want to grow you toward bigger work' promises development, and development is deliverable.

If Someone names a real colleague while describing their bench: Protect them warmly and immediately: 'Hold that. Roles, never names.' Bench membership is sensitive people data; it never gets discussed by name on a group call.

Tough questions, ready answers

Q: Won't a succession conversation make my best person start job hunting?
The research runs the other way: people leave when growth is invisible, and a bench seat makes it visible. If the worry stays with you, the stay-interview practice lab attached to this month's LinkedIn Learning course, Retaining Your Best Talent, was built for exactly this.

Q: What if I build a bench and the role never opens?
Then the person still got stretch work, new skills, and proof that someone invested in them, and the Transfer Portal gives those skills somewhere to go inside Vanderbilt. A bench that never gets promoted still retains and still grows.

Q: Do I need my own manager's approval to build a pipeline for my role?
Loop them in, because a manager developing successors is good news upward. What needs no approval: growing skills on your team and being honest with people about direction. Start there.

The bench is a gift Full 8 min · Core 6

Install the reframe: succession planning is growth planning for the people named to the bench.

Say

“Succession is the word managers whisper, because everyone in earshot hears replacement. Here is the reframe this whole month stands on: a bench is a list of people someone is betting on, and a seat on it is a gift. Growth work, visibility, a named direction. We whisper it, and we should be handing it over like good news.”

Do

  1. Read the concept card aloud once, slowly; it carries the reframe.
  2. Run the knowledge check; everyone answers on their own screen.
  3. Run the breakout: say the bench sentence out loud, gift or threat, 90 seconds each way.

Ask

“When you were first put on a bench or a plan yourself, how did you hear it?”

Expect:

  • Stories of it landing as a vote of confidence
  • Stories of quiet panic about being managed out
  • Some were never told, and found out later

Respond: All three answers teach the same thing: the framing decided the feeling. That framing is yours to control for your team.

Debrief: The plan is the same document either way. The framing decides whether it retains someone or scares them.

Watch for: Managers treating succession as a form for HR. Redirect to the person: what does the seat give them this quarter?

Transition: “The reframe only survives contact if the opening lines are right. That is next.”

Core path: Core: skip the breakout; ask for gift-or-threat votes in chat instead.

The fearless opening Full 10 min · Core 7

Train ears to hear which openings land as investment, which trigger replacement fear, and which are too vague to land.

Say

“Nobody remembers slide four of a succession conversation. They remember the first two sentences, and they decide in those two sentences whether this is a promotion track or a pink slip. So we are going to train on openings. Same news, three framings, and only one of them earns the conversation.”

Do

  1. Run Gift or threat? live; everyone plays on their own screen.
  2. After round one, pause: ask who called it differently and why.
  3. Run the breakout: say your own opening line, partner reports the first feeling it raises.

Ask

“Why does the compliance opening, the HR-needs-a-name one, do so much damage?”

Expect:

  • It makes the person a box to fill
  • It suggests the decision already happened somewhere else
  • It attaches their role to someone else's name

Respond: Right. It contains no growth and no future for the listener. Every strong opening starts with the person, never the chart.

Debrief: Openings about their growth raise energy. Openings about the org chart raise fear. Vague warmth raises nothing.

Watch for: Managers drafting openings that promise a promotion. Growth language must stay honest: readiness and direction, never a guarantee.

Transition: “You can open it for your team. Now the harder one: your own chair.”

Core path: Core: run the trainer, skip the breakout, take one voice on the debrief question.

Your own pipeline Full 9 min · Core 6

Get every manager to start a skills-first, multi-person pipeline for their own role and say it out loud.

Say

“Here is the move that separates managers who talk succession from managers who build it: naming a pipeline for your own role. It feels risky, and it reads as the opposite. A leader with a bench looks secure, gets freed for bigger work, and models the exact reframe we just taught. Skills first, more than one name, and the people on it get told.”

Do

  1. Run Start your pipeline; two minutes to make both choices and run it.
  2. Ask one volunteer to read their outcome aloud, weak or strong.
  3. Point at the pattern: skills first, several people, growth that starts now.

Ask

“What stops managers from naming successors for their own role?”

Expect:

  • Fear of looking replaceable
  • Fear the person will expect a promotion
  • Nobody on the team feels ready

Respond: Name the flip: a role only you can do is a role you can never be promoted out of. The bench is your own mobility plan too.

Debrief: A secret bench grows nobody. Skills first, several names, and the growth conversation happens this quarter.

Watch for: Managers picking one heir apparent. Push for two or three; a single name recreates the replacement drama on a new person.

Transition: “Quick recap, then you build your own bench card.”

Core path: Core: everyone runs the builder solo; skip the read-aloud.
