Section 01 · The Reframe

Succession planning is growth planning.

Strip the org-chart drama away and a succession plan is a list of people getting ready for bigger work. Being named to a bench means someone senior is betting on your growth. That is the version your team deserves to hear from you first.

The reframe

A succession plan answers one question: who is getting ready for bigger work, and what do they need next? Read it that way and the bench stops being about exits. It becomes the most concrete growth plan most people will ever be given.

Quick check: what does being named to a bench actually mean for the person on it?

On your own: Write the sentence 'you are on the bench for X' for one person on your team. Does it read as a gift or a threat?

Section 02 · The Conversation

Open it so they hear investment.

The same news lands as a promotion or a pink slip depending on the first two sentences. Openings that start with the org chart trigger the fear. Openings that start with the person's growth earn the conversation you wanted.

Play · Gift or threat?

Why this matters: Each opening is real manager language. Call how it lands in the listener's chest before any explaining starts.

On your own: Write your opening line for a succession conversation, then reread it as the listener. What is the first thing you would feel?

Section 03 · The Move

Your role needs a pipeline too.

The bravest succession move is naming who could grow into your own chair. It signals security, models the reframe, and frees you for bigger work. Build the two decisions that start it: who goes on the bench and what you say to them.
