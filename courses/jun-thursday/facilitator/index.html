05 · When things go sideways

If it breaks, you have a move

If Breakout rooms are unavailable: Run the breakout prompts as 60-second chat storms: everyone types their answer at once, you read the best three aloud.

If Emotions surface as the season closes: Let it breathe. Invite one-line reflections in chat on the year, read two aloud, and land the close warmly; a season close is allowed to feel like one.

06 · Tough questions, ready answers

Say each answer out loud once before you teach

Isn't deciding at 70 percent risky in a university, where mistakes are visible?

The rule travels with the door test. One-way, high-visibility calls still earn full diligence. The everyday calls we circle, the tools, drafts, meetings, and asks, are two-way doors, and the visible mistake there is usually the six-month delay, which everyone notices too.

Can I use AI to help me decide?

As an input, yes, inside the light: green for public or generic questions, yellow for internal content in approved VU tools, and red, never, for private information about people. AI can widen your options and pressure test your reasoning; the call itself stays yours. Deciding is the habit we spent all year protecting.

What happens to Anchor's Edge after this session?

The series year wraps, and Anchor's Edge comes back with room to grow. Your 90-day plan from Tuesday carries you to September, the capstone lab and this month's LinkedIn Learning course run through June, and the dashboard stays open for revisits. The habits were always the product.

Section 01 · The 70 Percent Rule

Decide at about 70 percent.

Waiting for full information is itself a decision: it keeps the status quo and calls it research. Jeff Bezos told Amazon shareholders most decisions should be made with about 70 percent of what you wish you knew. Acting teaches the rest.

The 70 percent rule

Decide when you have about 70 percent of the information you wish you had. Below that, you are guessing; near 100, you are late, and the choice has usually been made for you. Acting generates the information that waiting never will.

In the room

In the room: bring back the decision you carried in today, the one you have been circling. As the rule sinks in, weigh that decision honestly: what do you already know, what are you still waiting for, and when did truly new information last arrive? For most circling decisions, that was weeks ago.

Talk it through: What was your longest-circled decision this year actually waiting for, and did that thing ever arrive?

Quick check: what does the 70 percent rule actually claim?

Grounded in: The 70 percent rule, Jeff Bezos, 2016 Amazon shareholder letter

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: name a decision this year you delayed past the useful point. What was the wait actually buying? 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Name a decision this year you delayed past the useful point. What was the wait actually buying?

Go deeper

What counts as 70 percent

The number is a posture, not a measurement. A practical proxy: you are at 70 when you can name the options, the worst plausible outcome of each, and what you would try first if forced to choose today. What you usually lack is certainty about other people's reactions, and no amount of research supplies that; only moving does. If you cannot name the options yet, you are below 70: gather. If you can, the gathering is done.

On your own

  1. For a live decision of yours, write the options, the worst plausible outcome of each, and your forced-choice pick. Notice what is actually missing.
  2. Write the one piece of information you are still waiting for and the date it last changed. If you cannot remember, you are past 70.

Expect:

  • Permission, or someone else deciding first

Respond: Both answers are the point: certainty rarely comes, and permission usually arrives after someone moves. The 70 percent rule is permission you give yourself.

Debrief

Section 02 · Doors and Speed

Match decision speed to the door.

Match the pace to the door. The image is Bezos's: two-way doors open again, so walk through and step back if it disappoints. One-way doors are hard to reopen, and they earn the extra data.

Play · Call the door

Why this matters: Three live decisions. Call each door before you pick a speed.

Grounded in: Two-way doors, Bezos 2015 shareholder letter

Breakout · 3 min: Pairs: name one live decision and call its door. 60 seconds each.

On your own: Name one live decision of yours and call its door. Then argue the other reading for a moment, honestly.

Go deeper

Doors that look one-way

Three things weld a reversible door shut in the mind: visibility, since people saw you choose and stepping back feels like failure; sunk effort; and a missing exit plan. The first two are about ego, not the door. The third is fixed in one line: name, before you walk through, what result by what date would send you back. A written exit condition makes any door easier to reopen, because stepping back was part of the plan.

On your own

  1. Take a call you have been treating as one-way and write its exit line: what result, by what date, sends you back through the door.
  2. Ask the second question too: is it yours? If not, draft the two-line recommendation to its real owner today.
Facilitator scriptFull 11 min · Core 8

Teach reversible versus one-way decisions and match the pace to the door.

Say

“The refinement that keeps the 70 percent rule honest comes from the same shareholder letters: decisions are doors. Most swing both ways. You can walk through, look around, and walk back, so those deserve speed. A few are one-way, and they earn your slow, careful diligence. The trap for smart people is treating every door as one-way, which feels rigorous and is really just circling.”

Do
  1. Run Call the door live; everyone plays on their own screen.
  2. After round three, pause on the third label: routing a decision to its owner counts as deciding.
  3. Run the breakout: one live decision, call the door, argue the other side.
Ask

“Why does round three, the decision that was never yours, matter so much?”

Expect:

  • A lot of circling is really unowned decisions
  • Sending the recommendation is a relief

Respond: Yes. Packaging your 70 percent as a recommendation and handing it to the owner closes the loop. Deciding includes deciding it is theirs.

Debrief

Two questions set the pace: could I step back from this, and is it mine? Speed for two-way doors, diligence for one-way, a memo for the rest.

Watch for: People classifying obviously reversible calls as one-way to justify the delay. Ask gently: what exactly could you never undo?

Transition

“Now the one you came in carrying: the decision you have been circling.”

Section 03 · The Deadline

Give the decision a deadline.

Everyone carries one decision they have been circling for months: a posting, a conversation, an ask. Name it, frame it as a fork with two options, and give it a deadline that one witness knows about. Then book 30 minutes on deadline day to make the call.

Practice · Land the decision

Why this matters: Take the decision you have been circling. Choose how you frame it and how you force the landing.

Choose both steps
Go deeper

After the landing

Two things deciders do that circlers skip. First, they write the call down with the date and top reason; when doubt returns weeks later, the note answers it, and if the world truly changed, it shows that too. Second, they expect the wobble: a decided fork keeps arguing for a day or two. Do not reopen it on feeling alone; reopen only if your written exit condition fires. The lightness after the wobble passes is the real payoff.

On your own

  1. Prepare your landing note now: the fork, the deadline, and a blank line for the call and its top reason. Keep it where your witness can ask about it.
  2. Decide in advance what evidence, not what mood, would reopen the decision. Write that one line under the note.
Facilitator scriptFull 9 min · Core 6

Convert one circled decision into a framed fork with a deadline, a witness, and booked time.

Say

“Everyone in this room is circling something, a posting or a conversation you keep rescheduling. The circling feels like progress because the decision stays technically alive. So we end the year by landing one. Frame it as a fork, set a date, tell a witness, book the half hour. You already have your 70 percent; June is the deadline.”

Do
  1. Run Land the decision; two minutes for both choices.
  2. Invite one volunteer to share their fork, options only, no backstory.
  3. Point at the pattern: named fork, real date, witness, appointment.
Ask

“What makes telling a witness so much stronger than a private deadline?”

Expect:

  • Private deadlines negotiate with you
  • Being asked about it later is gentle pressure that works

Respond: Exactly. A witness converts a promise to yourself into a promise, full stop. Choose someone kind and reliable; that is all it takes.

Debrief

Fork, date, witness, appointment. Any decision that fits that shape lands; the ones that resist the shape usually belong to someone else.

Watch for: Backstory spirals when people share. Keep shares to the fork and the date; the room does not need the case file.
