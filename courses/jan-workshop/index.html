Section 01 · The Pick

The right first chore is small and green.

A first win is chore-sized: something you can finish today and check quickly. Then the light gets the final say. Green, public or generic: go. Yellow, internal work: approved VU tools only. Red, private information about people: never.

Play · Strip it or skip it

Why this matters: Real chores arrive mixed, and the most sensitive piece decides the light for the whole task. Call each one before you run anything.

Breakout · 3 min: At your table: read your chore aloud and let the table call its light together. Anything mixed gets stripped to its green core before it runs.

Section 02 · The Run

Improve the draft with one follow-up.

Brief the tool like a colleague: the job, the details, the shape you want. Then run it in the approved VU tool in front of you. Ethan Mollick's research found the strongest users work in a back-and-forth with the tool. When the draft lands close, ask again.

Practice · The follow-up

Why this matters: The first draft came back stiff and a little generic. Choose what you do next.

Breakout · 3 min: Run your ask in the tool now; wave a navigator over anytime. When the draft lands, tell a neighbor the one change you would make, then say exactly that to the tool.

Section 03 · The Ship

A win is checked, then shipped.

The tool sounds just as sure when it is wrong, so MIT Sloan's rule is think first, verify always. Check every name, date, and number, make the voice yours, then ship it. Shipped work is what counts as a win.

The ship test

A first win has three parts: the check (every fact verified, the voice yours), the ship (the work sent, filed, or used), and the story (you can say what the check caught). A perfect draft sitting in a folder is a rehearsal.

In the room

Every screen in the room now holds a draft that did not exist an hour ago. This is where first tries usually stall: the draft feels done, the day moves on, and the folder keeps it. What remains is the finishing: check it with a neighbor, then ship it for real.

Talk it through: What did the check catch on your draft or your neighbor's? And where exactly is your draft about to go: sent to whom, filed where, used for what?

The recap, with the answers

1 · A chore mixes public content with one detail about a person's private situation. The call?

Answer: The private piece never goes in; strip it or skip it

Private information about people is red, always, and the most sensitive piece decides. Stripped to its generic core, the chore can run green.

2 · The first draft comes back close but stiff. The strongest next move?

Answer: Tell the tool what to change, plainly

Follow-ups are the cheapest skill in the room. Say what to change the way you would to a colleague, and the second draft arrives nearly done.

3 · What turns today's draft into a first win?

Answer: Checking it and shipping it as real work

Checked, then shipped: sent, filed, or used. The win is the work leaving your hands, plus the story of what the check caught.
