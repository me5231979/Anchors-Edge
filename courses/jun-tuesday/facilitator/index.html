05 · When things go sideways

If it breaks, you have a move

If Screen share or the site fails mid-session: Paste the learner link in chat and narrate from your own browser; every interaction runs on learner devices without the share.
If Breakout rooms are unavailable: Run the breakout prompts as 60-second chat storms: everyone types their answer at once, you read the best three aloud.
If Someone's chosen handoff task comes up yellow or red: Thank them for running the light honestly; that is the win. Yellow tasks can live in approved VU tools day to day; for the plan line, help them find the green task nearby: the generic template, the public draft, the reformat.
If A participant says their job has no AI-touchable tasks: Go smaller: a first draft, a summary, a reformat. If the light and the tools truly rule everything out, their plan leans on the skill and habit lines, which is still a complete plan.

06 · Tough questions, ready answers

Say each answer out loud once before you teach

Is it safe to put my 90-day plan into an AI tool to improve it?

Run the light. The plan as written here is generic, task-level text about your own work: green territory, and an approved VU tool is always the safer default. The moment a plan mentions colleagues, performance, or any private detail about a person, that part is red: never.

What if I hand a task to AI and the quality drops?

That is what the day-30 review is for: catch it while the plan is alive. Keep the handoff as a first-draft step with your own review on top; quality usually recovers when the human stays as editor. If it does not, swap the task, and the plan still stands.

Ninety days from June lands in September. Does the plan survive summer schedules?

That is exactly why the plan is three lines instead of ten, and why the review sits at day 30, before the heaviest vacation stretch. A small plan bends around a summer; an ambitious one breaks against it.

Section 01 · One Skill

Pick a skill you can finish.

The best 90-day skill goal is embarrassingly small. Prompting AI well for your weekly report beats mastering AI. Reading one dashboard beats learning analytics. Small and finished builds more capability than ambitious and abandoned, every time.

The 90-day skill test

A good 90-day skill passes three questions: can I name the work I will use it on next week, can someone tell in September whether I built it, and can I practice it inside my actual job? If any answer is no, shrink the skill.

Quick check: which of these is the strongest 90-day skill goal?

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: trade one skill you could realistically build by September. Partner's job: shrink it until it honestly fits 90 days. 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Write one skill you could realistically build by September. Then shrink it until it honestly fits 90 days.

Facilitator scriptFull 8 min · Core 6

Shrink skill ambitions into one named, checkable, 90-day goal.

Say

“Nine months of AI learning could end with a shelf of good intentions. It is going to end with a plan instead, and the plan has exactly three lines. Line one is a skill, and the discipline is smallness: a skill you can name the work for, check by September, and practice inside your actual job.”

Do
  1. Read the 90-day skill test card aloud, all three questions.
  2. Run the knowledge check; let everyone answer on their own screen.
  3. Run the breakout: trade skills, then shrink each other's until they fit.
Ask

“What is the smallest useful version of the skill you just thought of?”

Expect:

  • A named task with a tool attached
  • Some resistance; the ambitious version feels better

Respond: Honor the ambition, then aim it: the big version is the direction, the small version is the plan. September can only check the small one.

Debrief

Named work, checkable by September, practiced inside the job. Any skill that passes those three questions is plan-ready.

Watch for: Goals stated as courses to take. Courses feed skills; ask what task the course serves.

Section 02 · One Task

Hand AI a task, once the light turns green.

The plan's second line is a task you stop doing by hand. For your plan, pick a green task: public or generic, go. The full light still governs the rest: yellow is internal, approved VU tools only; red is private information about people, never.

Play · Clear the handoff

Why this matters: Three tasks people wanted to hand to AI this year. Run each through the traffic light before anything moves.

Breakout · 3 min: Breakout pairs: name the task you want to hand to AI and run it through the light out loud. Partner checks the call. 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Name the task you want to hand to AI and run it through the light. Write the color next to it.

Facilitator scriptFull 10 min · Core 7

Choose a green handoff task and clear it through the traffic light.

Say

“Line two is a task you hand to AI. For the plan we keep it green: public or generic, go. The rest of the light still governs your week: yellow means internal, approved VU tools only, and red means private information about people, never, in any tool, for any reason. The task can be small; the discipline cannot.”

Do
  1. Run Clear the handoff live; everyone plays on their own screen.
  2. After round three, restate red in one breath and let it land.
  3. Run the breakout: each person's real task through the light, out loud.
Ask

“Where do well-meaning handoffs usually go wrong?”

Expect:

  • Internal content in a personal tool
  • People data hiding inside an innocent-looking task

Respond: Both. The fix is the same: say the color out loud before the task moves. A handoff you cannot color is a handoff that waits.

Debrief

The plan's second line is two decisions: the task and its color. For the plan, green only; write both on the card.

Watch for: Anyone rationalizing a red task as anonymized. Names removed is still information about people: red, never.

Transition

“Two lines down. The third line is the one you keep.”

30-min core: Core: run the trainer, skip the breakout, one voice on the debrief.

Section 03 · One Habit

Choose what stays yours.

The third line is a quiet promise: one habit you keep doing yourself, on purpose. The thank-you written by hand, the hard message drafted alone, the first read of a colleague's work. Naming what stays human keeps you the one steering.

Practice · Assemble the plan

Why this matters: A plan survives on how it is written and how it is reviewed. Choose both.

Facilitator scriptFull 9 min · Core 6

Name a deliberate human habit and assemble the dated, reviewable plan.

Say

“The third line is different: it is the thing you keep doing yourself, on purpose. Everyone has work where the doing is the point: the hard message, the handwritten thanks, the first read of a colleague's draft. Naming yours keeps the plan honest. Then we assemble all three lines into something with dates and a witness.”

Do
  1. Ask for human-habit examples in chat before the builder; read two aloud.
  2. Run Assemble the plan; two minutes for both choices.
  3. Point at the pattern: dated lines, early review, a witness.
Ask

“Why does a plan about using AI more include a line about using it less?”

Expect:

  • It keeps judgment sharp on the work that matters
  • It makes each handoff a choice you made

Respond: Right. The habit line is proof you are steering. People who name what stays human hand off the rest with much less anxiety.

Debrief

Three lines, all dated, reviewed at day 30 with a witness. That is a plan a real September can grade.

Watch for: Plans with five skills and three handoffs. More lines mean fewer finishes; hold them to three.
