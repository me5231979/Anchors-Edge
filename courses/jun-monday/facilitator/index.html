05 · When things go sideways

If it breaks, you have a move

If Screen share or the site fails mid-session: Paste the learner link in chat and narrate from your own browser; every interaction runs on learner devices without the share.
If Breakout rooms are unavailable: Run the breakout prompts as 60-second chat storms: everyone types their answer at once, you read the best three aloud.
If A manager says every insight on their map points to hire: Run the order out loud: what would reskill look like here, what would redeploy look like, what would redesign look like? Hire earns its place only after those three answers exist.
If Someone names a real person while matching an insight to a move: Protect them warmly and immediately: 'Hold that. Roles, never names.' The moves work on work, and the traffic light is firm: private information about people is red, never.

06 · Tough questions, ready answers

Say each answer out loud once before you teach

Doesn't redesign just mean cutting jobs?

Redesign here means rebuilding a role around the work that stays human: judgment, exceptions, relationships. Done early and openly, it protects people from the quiet version, where the role hollows out and the person gets blamed for it. Position changes are a separate process with HR; this session is how you avoid needing one.

Can I use AI tools to analyze my team's data and pick the move?

Run it through the traffic light. Generic scenarios and public research: green, go. Internal workload data: yellow, approved VU tools only. Anything private about individual people, their performance, health, or circumstances: red, never. The move decision itself stays human; it is a judgment call about people you know.

What if my move needs budget or a requisition I don't control?

Then step one is the ask, owned and dated like any other step. A specific sentence with a role, a reason, and a date is exactly what gets funded; vague reskilling themes are what get deferred. Bring it to the action-planning lab and your HR consultant with the sentence already written.

Section 01 · The Vocabulary

Know your four workforce moves.

When AI changes work on your team, you have four moves. Reskill teaches the new version of the work. Redeploy points a strength where it is needed. Redesign rebuilds the role. Hire buys what you cannot build.

The order of operations

Try the moves in order of what you already have. Reskill and redeploy work with the people on your team today. Redesign reshapes the role itself. Hire is the move you make when the first three genuinely cannot cover the gap.

In the room

In the room: hold one real role from your team in mind, one where AI changed the routine work this year. Walk it through the order: reskill and redeploy use the people you have, redesign reshapes the role, hire covers what the first three cannot. Notice which move your instinct grabbed first.

Talk it through: Where has your team jumped straight to hire when reskill or redeploy could have covered it? What made the earlier moves hard to see?

Quick check: your exposure map shows AI now drafts most of a coordinator's routine summaries. What is the first move to consider?

Grounded in: Beyond hiring, McKinsey reskilling research (2020)

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: which of the four moves has your team used most in the last year, and which never? 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Which of the four moves has your team used most in the last year, and which never? Write both down.

Go deeper

When the order breaks

The order is a default, not a law. Reskill assumes the person wants the bigger work; some honestly do not, and that answer points you to redeploy. When the receiving work lives on another team, the Transfer Portal is the same move told honestly. A redesign that quietly doubles a role's scope is a hire in disguise. The order has evidence too: McKinsey's skill-gap surveys found reskilling usually beats hiring on cost and fit.

On your own

  1. Pick one role where AI changed the routine work. Write all four moves as one line each, even the two that feel wrong for this case.
  2. Cross out the moves the person or the budget truly cannot carry, then write one sentence on why the surviving move wins.
Facilitator scriptFull 9 min · Core 6

Install the shared vocabulary: reskill, redeploy, redesign, hire, and the order to try them in.

Say

“In April you built an exposure map: which work on your team AI touches most. Maps are not moves. This month gives the map a verb, and there are only four worth learning: reskill the person, redeploy the strength, redesign the role, hire what you cannot build. The order matters as much as the list. When McKinsey surveyed companies closing skill gaps, reskilling the people they already had usually beat hiring on both cost and fit, which is why hire sits last today.”

Do
  1. Walk the four moves once, one line each; resist the urge to lecture.
  2. Read the In the room card aloud; everyone holds one real role in mind and names their instinct move silently.
  3. Lead the discussion prompt, two or three voices, then run the breakout: which move most, which never?
Ask

“Which of the four moves does your team reach for first, honestly?”

Expect:

  • Hire, because it feels like the real solution
  • Reskill, because training requests are easy to make

Respond: Most rooms admit to hire-first. The order flips that default: the cheapest moves use the people already on your team.

Debrief

Four verbs, tried in order of what you already have. That order is the discipline of this month.

Watch for: Managers treating the moves as HR paperwork categories. Keep them as verbs a manager can start this week.

Transition

Section 02 · The Match

One insight, one move.

Read what the signal says about the work, then call the move it asks for. Hire is the reflex and the slowest door: CUPA-HR finds filling positions is supervisors' top challenge.

Play · Call the move

Why this matters: Three team insights, straight from an exposure map like the one you built this spring. Call the move each one asks for; hire stays off the board.

Grounded in: Higher ed supervisors on filling positions (CUPA-HR)

Breakout · 3 min: Breakout pairs: name one insight from your own team, role only, and call its move. Partner challenges it. 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Name one insight from your own team, role only, and call the move you would make. Then argue for a different move.

Go deeper

Reading a mixed signal

Real signals are rarely pure. Often the routine work shrank and the remaining work changed shape, so reskill and redesign both fit. Sequence them: redesign names the role's new shape first, then reskill grows the person into it. And if you cannot yet say what the role should look like in a year, the match is premature; watch the work for one more month with a date on the recheck. That is patience, not delay.

On your own

  1. Take the messiest insight from your exposure map and split it into two sentences: what shrank, and what grew or changed shape.
  2. Call a move for each sentence, then decide which one starts first and write the reason in one line.

Respond: Both true. Redeploy asks you to see strengths as portable. The Transfer Portal thinking from the fall applies inside your own team too.

Reskill grows the person with the work. Redeploy points a strength at new work. Redesign rebuilds the role. Say the reason out loud when you call the move; the reason is the match.

Section 03 · The Commit

A move needs an owner and a date.

A matched move becomes a workforce action when somebody owns the next step and the step has a date. Build that sentence now, specific enough that anyone could check later whether it happened.

Practice · Write the move

Why this matters: Take one insight-to-move match from your team and make it real. Choose how you state the move and how you anchor it.

Choose both steps
Go deeper

The second sentence

Your move sentence needs a companion most managers skip: the sentence you say to the person whose work is changing. Announced early, a move reads as investment; discovered late, the same move reads as verdict. Draft it in the week you date the move: what you saw change in the work, what you want to grow in them, and what stays theirs. The Oracle 1:1 template holds it well, and that conversation is usually step one anyway.

On your own

  1. Draft the three-part opener: what you saw change, what you want to grow, what stays theirs. Keep it to three sentences.
  2. Book the 1:1 where you will say it, inside the same week as your move's dated step one.

The Four Moves

Translating AI insights into workforce moves.

Goal 3 · AI-Enabled Workforce Readiness

What this session builds

  1. Name the four workforce moves: reskill, redeploy, redesign, and hire
  2. Match one AI-exposure insight from your team to the move it calls for
  3. Commit to one move with a named owner and a real date

The rule to remember

Match each AI insight to one of the four moves, then give it an owner and a date.

What each part taught

1 · Know your four workforce moves.

Try the moves in order of what you already have. Reskill and redeploy work with the people on your team today. Redesign reshapes the role itself. Hire is the move you make when the first three genuinely cannot cover the gap.

Grounded in: Beyond hiring, McKinsey reskilling research (2020)

2 · One insight, one move.

Good reading. The match between signal and move is exactly what your own exposure map needs next.

Grounded in: Higher ed supervisors on filling positions (CUPA-HR)

3 · A move needs an owner and a date.

A move in motion. The sentence is specific, the owner is named, and Friday makes it real.

The recap, with the answers

1 · AI has changed the work in a role on your team. What are your four moves?

Answer: Reskill, redeploy, redesign, hire

Reskill the person, redeploy the strength, redesign the role, hire what you cannot build. In roughly that order of cost.

2 · A strong performer's routine work is half automated, and another part of the team is understaffed. Best first move?

Answer: Redeploy the freed-up strength

A freed strength next to a starving need is the classic redeploy: fast, cheap, and it tells your best person their value survives the automation.

3 · What turns a matched move into a workforce action?

Answer: An owner and a date

Name the move, give step one an owner, and put a date on the calendar. Everything else is documentation.

Your commitment

Take the sharpest AI insight from your team, role only, never a name. Match it to its move and give it an owner and a date. If none of the three fits, the move is hire; take that one to your HR consultant. Nothing you type is saved or sent.

  • The reskill move. Grow the person toward the work that got bigger: judgment, exceptions, relationships. Book the first conversation.
  • The redeploy move. Point the freed-up strength at work that needs it. Name the receiving work and make the introduction.
  • The redesign move. Rewrite the role around what stays human, then staff the new shape. Draft the one-paragraph new role description.
