The goal this month serves

Goal 1, Talent Marketplace & Transfer Portal.

Audience

All Vanderbilt staff in the Anchor's Edge weekly rhythm; no management experience assumed. Works for 6 to 40 on a call; breakouts run in pairs. Builds on the Transfer Portal month earlier in the series; February is the Retain, Grow, Move month on succession and internal mobility.

Room setup

Virtual, instructor led: camera on, deck link in chat, breakouts of 2 available. Learners follow on their own screens via the welcome-slide link or QR. Nothing typed into the deck is saved or sent.

Materials

  • Meeting platform with screen share and breakout rooms; deck link ready to paste in chat
  • Facilitator machine with the facilitator edition open; notifications off
  • Learners on their own devices with the learner link
  • Chat open for share-outs; the capstone copy button pastes there cleanly

A week before

  1. Run the learner edition start to finish and do every interaction honestly; you will demo at least one live.
  2. Read this month's theme page on the Anchor's Edge dashboard so the goal tie-in is fluent.
  3. Send the invite with the learner link and one line on what to bring.

The day before

  1. Test screen share with the facilitator edition; confirm the notes rail is readable.
  2. Open the learner link on a second device; the QR must land there.
  3. Run the section interactions once so you can drive them without reading.

30 minutes before

  1. Welcome slide up as people join; link pasted in chat.
  2. Close every other tab; silence the machine.
  3. Breakout rooms pre-configured in pairs.

When things go sideways

If Screen share or the site fails mid-session: Paste the learner link in chat and narrate from your own browser; every interaction runs on learner devices without the share.

If Breakout rooms are unavailable: Run the breakout prompts as 60-second chat storms: everyone types their answer at once, you read the best three aloud.

If Someone asks a Portal or Marketplace mechanics question beyond today's scope: Park it visibly: 'Good question, logging it for the program team.' Point them to the Transfer Portal month materials in the series library and send parked questions upward with the pulse.

If A participant says their manager punishes people for looking internally: Take it seriously and keep it roles-only: 'If that is happening, there are channels for it, and I will point you to them after.' Restate the policy plainly; never litigate a specific manager on the call.

Tough questions, ready answers

Q: Will my manager be notified if I update my profile or get matched?
Your profile is yours, and keeping it current is normal professional upkeep, expected of everyone this month. Best practice is to bring your named direction to your manager yourself; a manager who knows your direction can advocate for it.

Q: Is this just a way to move people into jobs nobody wants?
The matching starts from your skills and your named direction, and you can always say no. Surfacing is an offer; nothing about these systems drafts anyone anywhere.

Q: I have been in the same role for fifteen years. Is this really for me?
Especially for you. Long tenure usually means a deep skill set and a thin profile. The skills sweep is where fifteen years of capability finally gets written down, and stretch projects surface for exactly that depth.

“Why do people leave the direction field blank?”

  • Fear of seeming disloyal or restless

Moving is normal Full 9 min · Core 6

Retire the loyalty myths and state internal mobility as a normal, encouraged Vanderbilt career path.

Say

“None of the tooling matters if people believe using it is dangerous. So hear it stated plainly: internal mobility is a normal, encouraged career path at Vanderbilt. A move across the university keeps your talent, your relationships, and your institutional knowledge here. The alternative to an internal move is usually an external one, and everybody loses that trade.”

Do

  1. Run Myth or normal? live; everyone plays on their own screen.
  2. After round one, pause: ask who has heard that myth somewhere, show of hands or chat.
  3. Run the breakout on what people have heard about applying internally.

Ask

“What would make you hesitate to make an internal move, honestly?”

Expect:

  • My manager's reaction
  • Feeling disloyal to my team
  • Not feeling qualified enough

Respond: Name each one back to the room. The manager fear is the myth we just retired; the qualification fear is why matching runs on skills, never titles.

Debrief: Myths punish ambition, and they only survive in silence. Vanderbilt is saying the opposite out loud: move, grow, stay.

Watch for: Anyone hearing this as a promise of promotions. The commitment is visibility of opportunity and safety to pursue it, never a guaranteed yes.

Transition: “Quick recap, then you build your next-move card.”

Core path: Core: run the trainer, skip the breakout, take one voice on the ask question.
