Put your growth on the record.

Your growth has a paper trail problem. The goals you set in a good career conversation live in your head, a notebook, or nowhere. This session moves them into the system, where they survive busy seasons and where opportunity can actually find them.

By the end of this session, you will be able to

  1. Enter one real development goal in the talent system, written so a reader can act on it
  2. Keep a short, useful note after any career conversation you are part of
  3. Sort what belongs on your visible record, in private notes, or nowhere at all

Section 01 · The Why

Invisible goals go unfed.

When a stretch project, a course seat, or an internal opening appears, someone looks at the record to see who is reaching for what. A goal that lives in your head is invisible to that search. Writing it into the system is how you raise your hand in advance.

The visibility rule

Your growth record is a standing hand-raise. Managers, mentors, and internal recruiters can only work with what they can see, so a goal in the system keeps advocating for you in rooms you are never in.

You want to move toward data work within two years. Where does that ambition do the most for you?

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: name a goal you are carrying that exists nowhere in writing. What would change if the right person could see it?

On your own: Name a goal you are carrying that exists nowhere in writing. What would change if the right person could see it?

Section 02 · The Sort

Some notes belong in the system. Some never will.

You will sit in career conversations all year: 1:1s, mentoring chats, peer feedback. Some of what you hear belongs on your visible record, some in your private notes, and some should never be written anywhere. The sort takes five seconds once you know it.

Play · System, notes, or nowhere?

Why this matters: After each conversation, you decide where the takeaway lives. Read each one and sort it.

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: one thing you would happily put on your visible record, and one thing you would keep in private notes. Compare sorts.

On your own: Write one thing you would put on your visible record and one thing you would keep in private notes.

Section 03 · The Entry

Write it so a reader can act.

A growth record is only as useful as its writing. 'Improve communication' gives a reader nothing to act on. A goal with a direction and a dated first step, and a note that holds the agreement, turn your record into something projects and people can answer.
