Section 01 · The Follow-Up

Point the tools at your own career.

Earlier in the series you learned the Transfer Portal as a system. Now point it at your own career. Deloitte's work on internal talent marketplaces names the payoff: people get matched to work by skills and aspiration, not by luck.

The planning kit

Read the two systems as one kit. The Talent Marketplace grows you where you are, through your skills profile and stretch work. The Transfer Portal carries the move once you are ready. A real plan uses both, in that order.

In the room

Everyone in this room already knows the Transfer Portal; the series taught it in depth. What most people have never done is sit down with the Marketplace and the Portal open and ask the selfish question: what is my plan? Today is that hour, with the university's explicit blessing.

Talk it through: When you learned the Portal earlier in the series, what did you privately wonder about your own next move? Say the honest version out loud.

Quick check: you want a growth plan, not just a job search. Where does it start?

Grounded in: Activating the internal talent marketplace (Deloitte)

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: what do you want these systems working on for you this year, a skill, a project, a move? One pair shares.

On your own: Write down what you want these systems working on for you this year: a skill, a project, or a move.

Go deeper

Growth first, move second

The commonest planning mistake is starting with a destination role and working backward. Roles change, get frozen, or go to someone readier. Directions are sturdier than destinations: name the kind of work and the skills it runs on, then let the Marketplace grow you toward it while the Portal watches for openings shaped like it. When a role finally surfaces, you are applying from readiness instead of hope.

On your own

  1. Write your direction as one sentence: the kind of work, not a job title. Test it: would three different real roles fit inside it?
  2. List the two skills that direction runs on which you cannot evidence yet. Those are your Marketplace growth targets.
  3. Open the Transfer Portal once this week and note which current openings rhyme with your direction, even ones you are not ready for.

Section 02 · The Readiness

Write a profile the matching can read.

Matching is only as good as what it can read. Two moves keep you findable: a Talent Marketplace profile that says what you can actually do, and a direction named in writing. Build both here.

Practice · Build your findability

Why this matters: Two entries decide what the matching can see. Choose how you write your skills and how you name your direction.

Choose both steps
Go deeper

The evidence line

A skill without an evidence line is a keyword, and keywords are cheap. The strongest lines have three parts: what you did, the scale or the stakes, and what it produced. 'Process mapping: redesigned intake during our busiest cycle and cleared the backlog' is credible, findable, and easy for an advocate to repeat. Skills from past roles count in full; the matching has no way to discount where a capability came from.

On your own

  1. Take your three strongest skills and write the three-part evidence line for each: the thing, the stakes, the result.
  2. Add one skill from a past role or outside work that your current job never uses, with its own evidence line.
  3. Read each line as a stranger. If it could describe anyone with your title, sharpen it until it could only describe you.

Section 03 · The Culture

An internal move is a career path.

Hear the policy plainly: internal mobility is a normal, encouraged career path at Vanderbilt. LinkedIn's research finds people stay nearly twice as long where moving inside is easy.

Play · Myth or normal?

Why this matters: Sort each statement: an old myth to retire, a normal move to support, or a readiness gap to fix. Ask what each one would do to a real career.

Grounded in: Internal mobility and retention, LinkedIn Workplace Learning research

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: what have you heard, in any workplace, about what happens to people who apply internally? Myth or real, 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Write down one thing you have believed about applying internally. After this section, decide: myth or real?

Go deeper

The manager conversation, scripted

The myths mostly die in one specific conversation: telling your manager you are looking at a move. Plan it in three beats. The direction: what you are growing toward and why. The timing: nothing is decided, you are being deliberate, not restless. The continuity: what you would do to land your current work well. Managers who hear all three usually turn into advocates, because you have just shown them exactly how to support you.

On your own

  1. Draft the three beats for your own manager conversation: direction, timing, continuity. Two sentences each.
  2. Say the direction beat out loud once. If it sounds like an apology, rewrite it until it sounds like a plan.
