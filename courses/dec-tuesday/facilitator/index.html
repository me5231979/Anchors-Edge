05 · When things go sideways

If it breaks, you have a move

If Screen share or the site fails mid-session: Paste the learner link in chat and narrate from your own browser; every interaction runs on learner devices without the share.
If Breakout rooms are unavailable: Run the breakout prompts as 60-second chat storms: everyone types their answer at once, you read the best three aloud.
If Learners lack Oracle access or cannot open it during the session: Nothing requires live Oracle. Demo from your own screen, and have people draft their agenda item and feedback lines in the deck; entering them takes two minutes after the call.
If Someone starts naming a colleague while describing feedback they want to give: Redirect warmly: roles, never names. The specifics belong in the tool, addressed to the person; the room only needs the shape of the example.

06 · Tough questions, ready answers

Say each answer out loud once before you teach

What if my manager never opens the template?

Your entry still works: it frames the meeting when you bring it up, and it timestamps that you raised it. Most managers start using the template the week their reports do. If it stays ignored, raise that kindly as its own agenda item.

Is feedback I give in Oracle visible to the person and their manager?

Treat everything in the tool as on the record; that is the point, because recorded recognition travels. Write anything you give as words you would happily say with the person in the room, since functionally you are.

I don't manage anyone. Is this month really for me?

Entirely. The manager standard tells you what to expect from your own manager, and the tools are two-sided by design; this month lands only if both chairs use them. If you are curious where the manager side goes, the New Manager Foundations course on LinkedIn Learning is open to everyone.

Section 01 · The Template

The template has two sides.

Open your next 1:1 in Oracle and you will find a shared agenda both people can edit. Most of us leave it blank and inherit whatever the meeting becomes. Typing in your two items the day before changes the meeting you get.

Your two items

Before every 1:1, type two items into the shared template: the blocker you need cleared and the growth topic you want time on. Typed the day before, they shape the agenda; raised in the last five minutes, they get sympathy and no time.

In the room

Think about your last three 1:1s: who typed the agenda, if anyone did? Oracle's shared template gives both chairs the same editable page, and the habit that changes the meeting is small. Two items, typed the day before: one blocker you need cleared, one growth topic you want real time on.

Talk it through: Why do so few of us type our own items into a shared agenda, and what would change in your 1:1s if you always did?

Quick check: when does your agenda item do the most work?

Breakout · 3 min: In pairs: what item have you been saving for the right moment instead of typing into a 1:1 agenda? 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Write down the one item you have been saving for the right moment. That is your first template entry.

Go deeper

Wording items your manager can prepare for

The wording of an item changes what the meeting can do with it. Career development is a topic; time on the grants project rotation and what it would take is an item your manager can prepare an answer for. For a sensitive subject you would rather not spell out in the record, type the headline and hold the detail: ten minutes on team workload. The template needs enough to prepare on, and nothing more.

On your own

  1. Draft your two items for your next 1:1: the blocker with the decision it needs, and the growth ask with a concrete next step attached.
  2. Reread each item as your manager. Could they prepare an answer from the words alone? Sharpen until yes, then type both into Oracle.

Section 02 · The Tools

Feedback now has a front door.

Oracle gives you two moves anyone can make: request feedback on your work from people who saw it, and give feedback that lands in a colleague's record. Both work best specific and fresh. The skill is knowing which move a moment calls for.

Play · Pick the move

Why this matters: Three moments from an ordinary week. Choose the move that turns each one into something on the record.

Breakout · 3 min: Breakout pairs: whose recent work deserved recorded feedback from you and got a chat message instead? Roles only. 90 seconds each way.

On your own: Name one colleague, role only, whose recent work deserved recorded feedback from you. That is your first give.

Go deeper

Specific enough to act on

Both tools live or die on specificity. A request that asks any feedback on my presentation? invites politeness. One that asks was the budget section clear enough to decide from? gets an answer you can use. Gives work the same way: name the exact work and its effect. Your data cleanup saved the Friday deadline travels further than great job, because anyone reading the record later can see what actually happened.

On your own

  1. Draft one feedback request about specific recent work: the piece of work, the person who saw it up close, and the one question you want answered.
  2. Draft one give, role only for now: the exact thing a colleague did and the effect it had on your work. Send both through Oracle this week.
Facilitator scriptFull 10 min · Core 7

Teach when to request feedback, when to give it, and when a topic belongs on the 1:1 agenda instead.

Say

“Oracle gives everyone two feedback moves. Request: you ask people who saw your work how it landed, and their answers join your record. Give: you put specific recognition or input into a colleague's record, where it travels further than chat ever will. The skill is matching the moment to the move, so let's practice on three.”

Do
  1. Run Pick the move live; everyone plays on their own screen.
  2. After round two, ask in chat who has ever received recorded feedback from a peer; notice the energy.
  3. Run the breakout on the colleague whose work deserved a recorded give.
Ask

“Why does recorded feedback beat the same words in chat?”

Expect:

  • It reaches their manager
  • It survives to review season
  • Chat scrolls away

Respond: All three. The words might be identical; the address is different. Feedback in the record shows up when decisions about people get made.

Debrief

Request when you need eyes on your work. Give when a colleague earned it. Anything needing real discussion goes on the 1:1 agenda.

Watch for: Worry that requesting feedback signals weakness. Reframe it: asking specific people about specific work reads as someone serious about getting better.

Section 03 · The Record

Make the record work for you.

A year from now, the strongest case for your growth is a trail: agendas you set, commitments you closed, feedback you asked for. That trail gets built one meeting at a time, and it starts with how you set up the next one.

Practice · Set up your next 1:1

Why this matters: Your next 1:1 is already on the calendar. Choose what you bring in and what you carry out.

Choose both steps
Go deeper

What the trail proves later

Picture the record after a few months of the habit. Agenda items show what you pushed for and when. Closed commitments show follow-through with dates attached. Requested feedback shows you invited scrutiny before anyone required it. Each entry is small on its own. Together they answer the review conversation before it starts, and they make the same case when a Transfer Portal opportunity gets serious.

On your own

  1. Find the one commitment from your last 1:1 that should have made a shared record. Write it as a line with an owner and a date.
  2. Type it into the template for your next 1:1 as the first check-in item, so your trail starts with something that closes.
Facilitator scriptFull 9 min · Core 6

Convert template and feedback habits into a growth trail: agendas in, commitments out.

Say

“Play the long game for a second. A year from now, someone will ask what you have grown into, in a review or an internal interview. The strongest answer is a trail: agendas you set, commitments that closed, feedback you asked for and acted on. Trails get built one meeting at a time, so let's set up your next one properly.”

Do
  1. Run Set up your next 1:1; give the room two minutes for both choices.
  2. Ask one volunteer to read their outcome aloud, weak or strong.
  3. Point at the pattern: typed beats mental, shared beats private.
Ask

“What does a shared commitment have that a private note does not?”

Expect:

  • Both names on it
  • It gets checked next meeting
  • Nobody can misremember it alone

Respond: Right. A private note protects your memory; a shared commitment obligates two calendars. Growth lives in the second kind.

Debrief

The record is your career's paper trail. Feed it: items in before, commitments out after, feedback along the way.

Watch for: Anyone treating the record as surveillance. The frame is ownership: this is your trail, built by you, arguing for you.
