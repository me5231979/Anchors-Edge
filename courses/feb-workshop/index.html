Section 01 · The Draft

Write it for one real person.

Beverly Kaye and Sharon Jordan-Evans built the stay interview on two questions: why do you stay, and what would make staying easier? Draft yours for one real person, role only.

The listening rule

The urge to fix each issue mid-interview is the classic mistake. Fixing turns the conversation into your to-do list and stops the telling. Keep asking, write down what you hear, and save the follow-through for the days after.

In the room

Every manager in this room drafted for someone specific. Before the role-play starts, hear the frame: the person across from you will say things you cannot fix on the spot, things about pay, workload, other people. Your practice partner will make sure of it. The skill is staying in the listening.

Talk it through: Think of the person you drafted for. What are you most afraid they will say when you ask, and what does that fear tell you about the conversation you have been avoiding?

Mid-interview, they name a frustration you could fix today. What is the strong move?

Grounded in: Stay interviews, Kaye and Jordan-Evans (Hello Stay Interviews)

Breakout · 3 min: At your table: read your opening aloud. The table answers with what the employee would feel first, then helps you sharpen it.

Section 02 · The Rounds

Say it out loud before it counts.

Reading a draft is not the skill; staying in the conversation is. Active practice is one of ATD's levers for moving training into real work, so run your opening on a partner playing the employee. Two moments will test you, and both have a strong move.

Practice · The hard moments

The rule to remember

Hold the stay interview: ask why they stay, listen, and follow through visibly.

What each part taught

1 · Write it for one real person.

The urge to fix each issue mid-interview is the classic mistake. Fixing turns the conversation into your to-do list and stops the telling. Keep asking, write down what you hear, and save the follow-through for the days after.

Grounded in: Stay interviews, Kaye and Jordan-Evans (Hello Stay Interviews)

2 · Say it out loud before it counts.

A round that sounded like a real conversation. The employee said something true, and you stayed in the listening.

Grounded in: Design Tactics for Training Transfer, TD Magazine (ATD)

3 · The answers are a to-do list.

You can sort what you hear. Act on yours, carry the rest upward out loud, and never let a warning wait.

Grounded in: Gallup, This Fixable Problem Costs U.S. Businesses $1 Trillion

The recap, with the answers

1 · What is a stay interview, run well?

Answer: A listening conversation about why they stay

You ask, you listen, you capture. The fixing comes afterward, visibly, which is what makes the listening believable next time.

2 · They go quiet after your best question. You should...

Answer: Let the silence sit, then invite more

Held silence is the answer forming. What gets said after it is usually the truest sentence of the whole interview.

3 · They name pay, which you do not control. The strong move?

Answer: Name your limits, advocate upward, ask what else

Honesty about your limits keeps trust, and visible advocacy is itself a retention lever. Then keep asking; pay is rarely the whole story.

Your commitment

You practiced it out loud. Now commit to the real conversation: the person, role only, and the window. Nothing you type is saved or sent.

  • The rehearsed interview. Run the opening and questions exactly as drafted and practiced today, and stay in the listening.
  • The listen-back. Close the real interview by reading back what you heard and naming the one thing you will act on first.
  • The follow-through. Within two weeks of the interview, act visibly on one thing they told you, and tell them you did.
