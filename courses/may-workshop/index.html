Section 01 · The Line

Say what the camera saw.

Before any round runs, every line gets the camera test: would a lens have caught it? 'You're careless' fails; no camera films careless. 'The last two summaries went out with old numbers' passes. Camera-true lines are checkable, hearable, and fixable.

Play · Camera or character?

Why this matters: Three feedback lines about to be delivered. Call each one before it leaves the mouth.

Breakout · 3 min: Write one character verdict you have heard at work, real or typical, then pass it left. Rewrite the verdict you receive as a camera-true line; read both versions aloud.

Section 02 · The Rounds

The face is the curriculum.

Now the rounds. In pairs, with the printed scenario cards: one gives, one receives, then swap. The words you drafted will change the moment a real face reacts, and that change is the lesson no screen can teach. Receive it cleanly: thanks, one question.

The room rule

Live feedback is a duet. The giver keeps the line camera-true and stops talking when it lands. The receiver offers one thank you and one clarifying question, no case for the defense. Both jobs take practice, and today both get it.

In the room

Watch what happens in this room during the rounds: givers softening lines mid-sentence when a face tightens, receivers rehearsing rebuttals instead of listening. Every wobble is the real skill announcing where it lives. The weekly sessions describe these moments; a room puts you inside one.

Talk it through: Which was harder just now, giving or receiving? What did your body do that surprised you, and what will you try in the next round?

Quick check: mid-round, your receiver starts explaining why it happened. What is the duet move?

Breakout · 3 min: Run the round with your scenario card, roles never names. Then debrief as a pair: the receiver names what landed, the giver names what the face across from them changed.

Section 03 · The Build

Feedback you hold becomes a plan you keep.

Everyone in this room holds real feedback: a 360 line, a pulse theme, something a colleague finally said. The one-page plan turns the sharpest piece into one commitment with a first rep and a date. Then a peer leans on it, because untested commitments bend.

Practice · Build the page

Why this matters: One page, one commitment. Choose how the commitment reads and what the pressure test asks.

Choose both steps

Breakout · 3 min: Trade plans with a table peer. Their job is one honest pressure test of your commitment: what would we see you doing differently, and by when? Revise on the spot.

The core idea

Feedback becomes a skill the first time it meets a real face and survives.
