Begin the session

Section 01 · Pass One

One line per role, written for a stranger.

Draw the grid. One line for each person on your team, role only, never a name, holding what they can do in the role and beyond it. The test of a good line: a stranger reading it could find that person a stretch project.

The stranger test

A map line works when a stranger could act on it. 'Senior analyst, solid' offers nothing. 'Senior analyst: grant budgeting, plain writing, wants facilitation' hands a stranger three moves. Write every line to that standard.

In the room

In the weekly session you practiced the inventory question one person at a time. The lab scales it: every role on your team gets a line, on one page, in the same sitting. The page is the discipline. If a line will not compress into capabilities, you do not yet know that person's skills.

Talk it through: Which line on your team will be hardest to write, and what does that tell you about where your attention has been going?

Quick check: which line belongs on your talent map?

Grounded in: Strengths and engagement research (Gallup)

Breakout · 3 min: At your table: read one of your map lines aloud, role only. The table calls it: could a stranger act on this line? Sharpen it together until the answer is yes.

Section 02 · Pass Two

Mark only what you can evidence.

Second pass over the map: a risk mark where ownership behavior has changed, a growth mark where someone has asked to build. Every mark needs its evidence written beside it, because marks without evidence turn a map into gossip.

Practice · Mark the line

Why this matters: Take the map line you are least sure about. Choose what earns a risk mark and what you write beside a growth mark; evidence is the standard for both.

Choose both steps

Breakout · 3 min: Table swap: trade one marked line with a neighbor, roles only. They challenge the evidence: is that a pattern change or one bad week? Defend it or downgrade it.

Section 03 · Pass Three

Circle one conversation.

A finished map points at more conversations than one week can hold. CUPA-HR finds feeling valued predicts staying better than pay does, and that is what one conversation buys. Circle the line that gets it first.

Play · Who gets the circle?

Why this matters: Three marked lines from three finished maps. Call what each one earns first.

Grounded in: CUPA-HR 2025 Higher Education Employee Retention Survey

Breakout · 3 min: Around the table, each manager reads their circled line, role only, with the reason in one sentence. Every table mate gets one challenge question: why this one first?

The core idea

Put evidence beside every mark, and act on the one line you circle.

The Talent Map Lab

The Know-Your-Team lab: build your first talent map.

Goal 1 · Talent Marketplace & Transfer Portal

What this session builds

  1. Build a one-page talent map of your team: one line per role, capability beyond the job title
  2. Mark flight-risk and growth signals on the map with evidence beside every mark
  3. Circle the first conversation the map earns and put a window on it

The rule to remember

Put evidence beside every mark, and act on the one line you circle.

What each part taught

1 · One line per role, written for a stranger.

A map line works when a stranger could act on it. 'Senior analyst, solid' offers nothing. 'Senior analyst: grant budgeting, plain writing, wants facilitation' hands a stranger three moves. Write every line to that standard.

Grounded in: Strengths and engagement research (Gallup)

2 · Mark only what you can evidence.

A map you can defend. Every mark carries its evidence, so every mark can become an action.

3 · Circle one conversation.

You can triage a map. Risk with evidence gets the circle, growth asks get fed, thin marks get watched.

Grounded in: CUPA-HR 2025 Higher Education Employee Retention Survey

Your commitment

Choose the map's first move and put a window on it. Roles only, never names; nothing you write here is collected.

  • The booked conversation. Put the circled conversation on the calendar before you leave the building, using the opening you built in this month's Monday session.
  • The finished map. Complete the lines you ran out of room for: every role gets a line that passes the stranger test, within the week.
  • The standing refresh. Put a monthly hold on your calendar to rewalk the map: marks re-evidenced, stale ones erased, one new circle.
